\subsection{Earlier intervention regimes}

\paragraph{Earlier regimes agree.}
Two further designs were run before the coefficient scale was calibrated, and we report them because
their agreement matters more than their individual power. At temperature $0.9$ with absolute rather
than normalised coefficients, $24$ matched comparisons against the placebo ladder all had intervals
crossing zero. Re-running at temperature $0.4$, which lowers the baseline \vendi{} from the ceiling
to roughly $4.7$ for pythia-2.8b and creates genuine headroom, produced the same result in all nine
comparisons. Lowering the temperature does not reveal an effect that the ceiling was hiding.


\subsection{Complementary placebos}

Ablation and injection were also run against the full placebo ladder in the first sweep, before the
coefficient scale was normalised. In the injection arm, all twenty-four matched comparisons between
the fitted direction and the random, shuffled, or orthogonalised control had intervals crossing zero,
with absolute changes bounded by $0.07$ \vendi{} except for one orthogonalised cell at $-0.165$ on
pythia-160m. In the ablation arm, eleven of twelve comparisons crossed zero and the twelfth pointed
against the hypothesis. These runs used absolute coefficients and are therefore individually less
informative than the calibrated sweep in \Cref{sec:interventions}, but their agreement with it across
a different coefficient regime is reassuring.

\subsection{Reproducing the numbers}

Each model's result directory contains the raw generations for every cell (\texttt{sweep\_cal.jsonl},
\texttt{sweep\_cal\_random.jsonl}, \texttt{local.jsonl}), the per-prompt split-half cosines
(\texttt{within\_prompt.jsonl}), the fitted directions (\texttt{directions.pt}), and the metric files
from which every table is computed. The analysis scripts that produce \Cref{tab:stability},
\Cref{tab:interventions} and the mixed-effects summaries read those files directly and require no GPU.
Generation itself was run on four RTX 4090 cards with a fixed seed per cell; the full sweep for the
seven headline models completes in under three hours on that hardware.

\subsection{Extended limitations}
\label{app:limitations}

\paragraph{Statistical power at the within-prompt level.}
The split-half test uses $32$ responses per prompt, so each half contributes about three responses
to each quantile. A larger sample could in principle reveal a weak but real signal. Two
considerations argue against this being the explanation for our result. First, the effect would have
to be small enough to escape detection at $16$ responses per half yet strong enough to drive a
steering effect, and the nulls in \Cref{sec:interventions} are correspondingly tight. Second, the
base models, where between-prompt structure is strongest and the estimator is best behaved, show the
same within-prompt failure as the instruction-tuned ones. A definitive test would raise the sample
to $128$ responses per prompt for a subset of models, which we leave to future work.

\paragraph{Metric dependence.}
\vendi{} is one diversity metric among several, and it is known to be biased at small sample sizes.
We mitigate this by using the same sample size in every cell, by centring embeddings within prompt,
and by reporting distinct-$2$, self-BLEU and repetition alongside. It remains possible that a
different metric would respond to the fitted direction, though the direction would then have to
affect a property our lexical and embedding measures both miss, which seems unlikely given that the
same direction produces no change in either.

\paragraph{Temperature and scale.}
All headline experiments run at temperature $0.9$, where \vendi{} baselines sit between $6.0$ and
$6.5$ out of $8$ and headroom is limited. We replicated at $0.4$, where pythia-2.8b's baseline falls
to $4.7$, and found the same nulls, but we did not replicate the full grid at low temperature for
every model. Our largest models have seven billion parameters; behaviour at frontier scale may
differ, and the coefficient calibration would have to be redone there because the residual norm
grows with width.

\paragraph{Intervention site.}
We intervene at a single middle layer in the main sweep, chosen before seeing the results. The
positive control shows that the effect of a labelled direction varies substantially with depth, so a
diversity effect could in principle be localised at a layer we did not probe. The five-layer sweep
for the positive control covers the range in which the model's behaviour is most plastic, and the
absence of any response there constrains how plausible a missed layer is, but we cannot rule it out.

\paragraph{Ethical considerations.}
The work uses open-weight models with publicly released prompts and requires no human annotation.
Its practical implication is cautionary: it argues against deploying diversity steering as a remedy
until the controls we describe have been passed, which if anything reduces the risk of false
confidence in a technique that does not work.

\subsection{Designing a positive result}
\label{app:positive}


Our results do not show that diversity is unmanipulable, only that it is not manipulable along a
direction estimated by contrasting dispersion quantiles. The ablation results point to a
formulation in which diversity is a property of a subspace rather than of a vector: removing the
span of a prompt's response set destroys the model's ability to branch within it, and the identity
of the contrast used to choose that subspace is irrelevant. A method that estimates the response
subspace directly---for instance from the principal components of a prompt's own generations---and
then intervenes on its spectrum rather than on a single direction would be a natural successor, and
it would be testable with exactly the controls we describe. Distributional interventions at the
sampler, such as the stochastic modulation of \citet{xu2026prism}, sidestep the representation
question entirely and are not threatened by our findings; the target of our critique is specifically
the linear-representation claim.
