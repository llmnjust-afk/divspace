\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Why the estimator succeeds at the wrong thing.}
The pooled split-half test is a natural sanity check, and it passes in a way that is actively
misleading. Two independent halves agree to three decimals on pythia-410m, which looks like strong
evidence for a real direction. What the agreement certifies is only that the statistic is stable,
and a statistic computed over responses to fixed prompts is stable whenever prompts have
characteristic activations, because the identity component dominates every average taken within a
prompt. The within-prompt test removes that component and the agreement vanishes. Reproducibility
therefore cannot validate a contrastive direction when the classes are quantiles of the model's own
output; the check must be run where the confound is absent.

\paragraph{Why prompt-local steering does not repair it.}
A natural objection is that pooling is the error and per-prompt fitting the fix. One prompt's own
direction performs exactly as well as another prompt's, and neither beats a random vector, because
\Cref{tab:stability} shows the within-prompt fit is itself unreproducible: the dispersion ordering of
a prompt's responses does not predict the ordering of a disjoint set of its own responses, so a
direction built from it is a random vector with extra steps. Base models look better only because
their between-prompt structure is stronger.

\paragraph{What would count as positive evidence.}
Our results do not show that diversity is unmanipulable, only that it is not manipulable along a
direction estimated by contrasting dispersion quantiles. The ablation results point to a subspace
formulation: removing the span of a prompt's response set destroys branching, and the identity of
the contrast used to choose that subspace is irrelevant. A method that estimates the subspace directly and intervenes on its spectrum, rather than on a
single direction, is the natural successor; \Cref{app:positive} develops the design.

\paragraph{Implications for practice.}
Three practices follow. Any claim that a direction controls diversity should report a within-prompt
split-half cosine; a pooled value alone is not evidence. The placebo ladder should include a matched
random vector and a quality gate, because our only significant effects came from perturbations that
also shortened and repeated the text and vanish when degenerate cells are excluded. A labelled
positive control is needed to show the intervention reaches the model, since raw direction norms
span $0.21$ to $161$ and absolute coefficients do not transfer across models.

\paragraph{Relation to negative results in steering.}
Prior work documents that steering vectors generalise poorly \citep{tan2024analyzing}, that
residual-stream addition can fail in dialogue \citep{kang2026promptactivation}, and that the pairs
dominate the vector's behaviour \citep{bohek2026detecting,hao2025patterns}. Our case is more
specific: the failure is not generalisation or pair quality but definition, since the target
quantity has no stable value for the unit at which the intervention is applied. No amount of extra
data or pair curation repairs that, and the split-half test detects it cheaply for any attribute
whose labels are constructed from model outputs rather than supplied by an annotator.
