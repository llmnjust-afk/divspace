\section{Limitations}

Our claim is deliberately narrow: a direction estimated by contrasting dispersion quantiles does not
support steering, in seven models at one temperature and one layer per model. We do not show that no
linear intervention can affect diversity, only that the estimator the contrastive recipe specifies
fails its own reproducibility prerequisite. The split-half test uses $32$ responses per prompt,
which is the smallest sample that keeps the fit non-degenerate; a larger sample could in principle
reveal a weak signal, though the surviving nulls in \Cref{sec:interventions} are tight
($|z| < 1.1$ after the quality gate) and the base models, where the estimator is best behaved, fail
the within-prompt test just as the instruction-tuned ones do. \vendi{} is one metric among several
and is biased at small samples, which we mitigate by holding the sample size fixed, centring
embeddings within prompt, and reporting lexical measures alongside. Finally, our largest models have
seven billion parameters and all headline runs use temperature $0.9$; frontier-scale behaviour and
other temperatures may differ, and the coefficient calibration would have to be redone there because
the residual norm grows with width.
